# Communication Guidelines — Sentences

<EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>

The basic principle is to make text easy to read and understand. Thus you must write short sentences and use a simple sentence structure. Instructions and descriptions each carry their own length limit — see the instruction and description documents. The passage-length before-and-after pairs are in the specimen documents.

## Short and clear sentences

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** write short, clear sentences that give specific information.
2. You **MUST** address the reader directly in instructions.
3. You **MUST** give each sentence in a description one topic only, then develop that topic gradually in the sentences that follow.
4. You **MUST** be accurate, and you **MUST** make the text show clearly how to do a task or how a system operates.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** write abstract text, and you **MUST NOT** give vague, general information.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** No leaks permitted. (This sentence is not very helpful. It does not tell the reader what to do.)
**Good:** Make sure that there are no leaks. (This sentence directly tells the reader what to do.)

</EXAMPLE>

## Complete sentences

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** give each sentence all of its parts, and you **MUST** write all words in full. A sentence with words missing is shorter but not easier to read.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** omit the noun. The reader will not know what you mean.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Can be a maximum of five inches long.
**Good:** Cracks can have a maximum length of five inches.

</EXAMPLE>

2. You **MUST NOT** omit the verb. The reader will not understand what the action is.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Rotary switch to INPUT.
**Good:** Set the rotary switch to INPUT.

</EXAMPLE>

3. You **MUST NOT** omit the subject. The reader will not know what you refer to.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** If installed, remove the shims.
**Good:** If shims are installed, remove them.

</EXAMPLE>

4. You **MUST NOT** omit the article to make the sentence shorter, because that can cause ambiguity. Where the article does not belong at all, the noun-cluster rules say so.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Remove the bolt and stop.
**Good:** Remove the bolt and the stop.

</EXAMPLE>

5. You **MUST NOT** use contractions — never "don't", "isn't", "aren't".

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** If your hands are wet, don't touch the USB power adapter.
**Good:** If your hands are wet, do not touch the USB power adapter.

</EXAMPLE>

## Vertical lists

A vertical list makes a long, complex sentence far easier to read. Use one when a sentence must carry many items — components, parts, documents — or many actions.

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** put a colon (:) at the end of the first sentence, before the first item.
2. You **MUST** identify each item with a number, a letter, a punctuation mark, or a symbol — a dash (-), a bullet point (•), a letter (a, b, c…), or a number (1, 2, 3…). Which marks to use is a matter for the applicable style guide.
3. You **MUST** start each item with an uppercase letter.
4. You **MUST** use an article before the noun that is the subject of each item, where one applies.
5. You **MUST** put a full stop at the end of an item that is a full sentence.
6. You **MUST** put a full stop at the end of the last item.
7. You **MUST** keep negative information visible in every vertical list, and this matters most in a safety instruction such as a warning or a caution. Repeat the negative command in every applicable item.
8. You **MUST** make each item connect clearly and correctly to the sentence before the colon.
9. You **MUST** keep the layout easy to read, and use the same indenture level for all items where possible.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** put a full stop at the end of an item that is not a full sentence.
2. You **MUST NOT** put a comma or a semicolon at the end of an item.

## Connecting words and phrases

A connecting word joins a topic in one sentence to an idea in the sentence that follows. In a description they structure the text logically. Available connecting words include "and", "but", "then", and "thus". Connecting phrases include "as a result" and "at the same time".

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** use connecting words and phrases to connect sentences that carry related topics.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** The localizer course aligns with the centerline of the runway. And the glideslope path is at a constant angle to the threshold of the runway.

</EXAMPLE>

You can also use pronouns and demonstrative adjectives to connect ideas in related sentences.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** Identify the disassembled parts with tags. This method will help you during the subsequent assembly procedures.

</EXAMPLE>

</EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>
