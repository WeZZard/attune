# Communication Guidelines — Verbs

<EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>

Rules for the form, tense, and voice of verbs. The passage-length before-and-after pairs are in the specimen documents.

## Verb forms and tenses

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** use only the forms that each verb settles on.

<EXAMPLE>

| Infinitive / Imperative | Simple present tense | Simple past tense | Past participle (as an adjective) |
|---|---|---|---|
| To decrease / Decrease | Decrease(s) | Decreased | Decreased |
| To give / Give | Give(s) | Gave | Given |

</EXAMPLE>

2. You **MUST** use those settled forms to make only these: the infinitive, the imperative (command form), the simple present tense, the simple past tense, the past participle as an adjective, and the future tense.

<EXAMPLE>

| Infinitive | Imperative (command form) | Simple present tense | Simple past tense | Past participle (as an adjective) | Simple future tense |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (To) Adjust | Adjust + object | You/we/they adjust | You/we/they adjusted | The adjusted linkage | You/we/they will adjust |
| | | It adjusts | It adjusted | | It will adjust |

</EXAMPLE>

3. You **MUST** place a past participle used as an adjective either before a noun, or after a form of the verb "to be" or "to become". Used this way it describes the condition of something, and it is not the passive voice. Do not use the past participle of a verb that has none: "been", the past participle of "to be", is never an adjective, and is always part of a complex verb form.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** Connect the disconnected wires. ("Disconnected" is an adjective before the noun "wires.")

</EXAMPLE>

4. You **MUST** treat a past participle that is itself settled as an adjective as available, even where the verb behind it is not. "Specified", "permitted", "completed", and "damaged" are adjectives in their own right.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** These areas have the specified damage limits.

</EXAMPLE>

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** use helping verbs to make complex verb structures. The present perfect (have/has adjusted), the past perfect (had adjusted), the present or past progressive (is/was adjusting), and every other complex form are excluded.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** The operator has adjusted the linkage. (The present perfect is not a settled tense.)
**Good:** The operator adjusted the linkage. (The simple past tense is settled.)

</EXAMPLE>

2. You **MUST NOT** write a complex passive — an auxiliary or modal verb ("can", "must", "will", "to be") with the infinitive of "to be" and a past participle.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** The sleeve will be adjusted.
**Good:** You will adjust the sleeve.

</EXAMPLE>

3. You **MUST NOT** use the "-ing" form of a verb, except as a technical name or as a modifier in a technical name. The "-ing" form can be part of a verb, an adjective, a noun, or a long string of modifiers and dependent clauses, and that ambiguity is why it is excluded.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** When you are doing this procedure, obey all the safety precautions.
**Good:** When you do this procedure, obey all the safety precautions.

Permitted as technical names, for example in procedural titles: Cleaning, Testing and Fault Isolation, Handling, Packaging, Shipping, Troubleshooting
Permitted as modifiers in technical names, where the "-ing" word is an adjective that describes the function of a system, component, part, tool, or equipment: Air-conditioning system, degreasing agent, grinding wheel, polishing disc, sanding machine, switching relay, welding torch
Permitted common words: the nouns lighting, opening, routing, and servicing, the adjectives mating, missing, and remaining, the pronoun something, and the preposition during.

</EXAMPLE>

## Active voice

In the active voice the subject does the action: A does B. In the passive voice the subject receives it: B is done by A. To test a sentence, ask "by whom or by what?" — if the text answers, the sentence is passive. The preposition "by" is a good indication, and its object is the agent you can promote to subject. A passive construction does not always name an agent.

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** use the active voice in instructions, always.
2. You **MUST** use the active voice in descriptions as much as possible. When you describe how a system is made or how it operates, the passive is sometimes necessary to keep the technical content correct. Use it only when it is really necessary.
3. You **MUST** know how to move a sentence out of the passive voice when you must. You can use one of these four methods.
   - Put the agent, usually the object of "by", at the beginning as the subject.
   - Change an infinitive verb to an active verb.
   - In instructions, change the verb to the imperative.
   - When the sentence names no agent, use "you" if the agent is the reader, or "we" if the agent is your own group.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** The test can be continued by the operator. (Passive)
**Good:** Continue the test. (Active)

</EXAMPLE>

## How to describe an action

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** use a verb, not a noun or another part of speech, to describe an action. Nouns describe actions abstractly. Verbs describe them clearly.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** The ohmmeter gives an indication of 450 ohms.
**Good:** The ohmmeter shows 450 ohms.

</EXAMPLE>

2. You **MUST** use a different construction when a word is not available as a verb.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Check the laptop battery.
**Good:** Do a check of the laptop battery.

</EXAMPLE>
