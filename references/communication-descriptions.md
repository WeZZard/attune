# Communication Guidelines — Descriptions

<EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>

A description gives information, not instructions. It can describe an item, a product, a system, or a component — its function, how it is made, and how it operates. It can give general information, as reports, brochures, and papers do. A note inside a procedure is a description too. The passage-length before-and-after pairs are in the specimen documents.

## Content structure

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** give information gradually, and give each sentence one topic only. Too much information too quickly forces the reader to read the text again.
2. You **MUST** use key words and phrases to organize the text logically. A key word occurs again and again in a text and connects different ideas. A key phrase does the same work.
3. You **MUST** organize the text so that its sentences connect logically. When you repeat the same key words and phrases, the sentences become logically connected. If you use many different words, your text will be more difficult to follow.

You can also use connecting words and phrases to help the reader follow the progression of ideas. They act as traffic signs, and tell the reader whether information is new, different, or a conclusion drawn from what came before — "and", "but", "then", "thus", "at the same time", "as a result".

## Sentences

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** use a maximum of 25 words in each descriptive sentence. Descriptive text carries more complexity than instructions, which is why its limit is higher.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** During the approach to the runway, deviation pointers in the course indicators show the pilot in which direction the aircraft must go. (22 words)

**Bad:** A smartphone is a cellular telephone that has an integrated computer and many other functions, such as an operating system, internet browsing as well as the ability to run software applications. (31 words)
**Good:** A smartphone is a cellular telephone that has an integrated computer and many other functions. (15 words) It includes an operating system and an internet browser, and it can also operate software applications. (16 words)

</EXAMPLE>

2. You **MUST** separate two topics that share one sentence, usually joined by "and". You can write a new sentence for each topic.

## Paragraphs

In an instruction, numbers and letters show the sequence of work steps. In a description, paragraphs hold related information together and organize the text into a logical sequence. A new paragraph tells the reader that new or different information starts here.

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** use paragraphs to show related information.
2. You **MUST** start each paragraph with a topic sentence that tells the reader the topic of that paragraph. The sentences that follow explain it or support it with additional information.
3. You **MUST** give each paragraph one topic only.
4. You **MUST** make the topic sentence give new information and connect it logically to the information before it. To do that, a topic sentence usually carries a key word, a connecting word, or a connecting phrase.
5. You **MUST** write topic sentences that carry the content. If the reader wrote down each topic sentence, they should make a good outline of the text. The reader finds specific information quickly that way.
6. You **MUST** write no more than six sentences in a paragraph. A paragraph longer than that no longer holds the reader's attention. Divide it into two.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** put different topics in the same paragraph.

</EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>
