# Communication Guidelines — Instructions and Safety Instructions

<EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>

An instruction tells the reader how to do a task. A safety instruction tells the reader that a task can be dangerous or cause damage. The passage-length before-and-after pairs are in the specimen documents.

## Instructions

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** use a maximum of 20 words in each instruction sentence.
2. You **MUST** hold warnings, cautions, and every other safety instruction to that same 20-word limit.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** Install the three auxiliary screws (2) in the flange of the motor assembly (9). (This sentence has 14 words.)

**Good:** CAUTION: WHEN YOU REMOVE THE SHROUD (26), BE CAREFUL NOT TO CAUSE DAMAGE TO THE SURFACE OF THE FLANGE ASSEMBLY (22). (This sentence has 20 words.)

**Bad:** Put preservation oil into the unit through the vent hole until the oil level is approximately 6 mm (0.24 inches) below the surface of the flange cover. (25 words)
**Good:** Put preservation oil into the unit through the vent hole. (10 words) Continue until the oil level is approximately 6 mm (0.24 in) below the surface of the flange cover. (16 words)

You cannot divide this instruction into two work steps, because the two actions occur at the same time.

</EXAMPLE>

3. You **MUST** write only one instruction in each sentence, unless two or more actions occur at the same time.
4. You **MUST** show the sequence of work steps clearly, usually with numbers or letters. Use as many work steps as the procedure needs.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** (1) De-energize the system. (2) Make sure that all of the switches on the control panel are in their correct positions.

**Bad:** Set the TEST switch to the middle position and release the SHORT-CIRCUIT TEST switch.
**Good:** A. Set the TEST switch to the middle position. B. Release the SHORT-CIRCUIT TEST switch.

</EXAMPLE>

5. You **MUST** put two actions in one sentence only when they occur at the same time.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** Hold the panel in its open position and install the fastener.
**Good:** Slowly extend the rod fully and make sure that it does not touch other parts.
**Good:** Cut and remove the wire.
**Good:** Remove and discard the seal.

</EXAMPLE>

You can also write more than one sentence in a work step: when the actions occur at the same time, or when a result occurs immediately after an action.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** Make sure that the locking torque of each of the four bolts (6) is a minimum of 0.30 Nm. Then, torque each of the four bolts (6) to 4.20 Nm.
**Good:** Measure the leakage from the outlet port. The leakage must not be more than 0.5 cc/minute.

</EXAMPLE>

6. You **MUST** write each instruction in the imperative (command) form. Any more complicated structure leaves the reader unsure whether the step is merely generally important, already done, or for a different person to do in the future.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** Set the switch to ON.
**Good:** Remove the four bolts.
**Good:** Increase the pressure to 60 psi.
**Good:** Inflate the tires.
**Good:** Install the new O-ring.

**Bad:** The test can be continued.
**Good:** Continue the test.

**Bad:** Oil and grease are to be removed with a degreasing agent.
**Good:** Remove oil and grease with a degreasing agent.

</EXAMPLE>

7. You **MUST** write the condition first when a work step is possible only under a special condition, then a comma, then the command. The comma is important, because it can change the meaning of the sentence.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Set the switch to NORMAL when the light comes on.
**Good:** When the light comes on, set the switch to NORMAL.

**Bad:** Apply the primer when the surface is dry.
**Good:** When the surface is dry, apply the primer.

**Bad:** Disconnect the constant speed drive (CSD) from the gearbox, if it does not operate correctly.
**Good:** If the constant speed drive (CSD) does not operate correctly, disconnect it from the gearbox.

These two sentences are both correct, and they mean two different things:
**Good:** If the constant speed drive (CSD) does not operate correctly, disconnect it from the gearbox.
**Good:** If the constant speed drive (CSD) does not operate, correctly disconnect it from the gearbox.

</EXAMPLE>

## Notes

A note carries descriptive text and only gives information that helps the reader during a procedure.

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** write a note only to give information.
2. You **MUST** keep each sentence in a note to a maximum of 25 words. A note can have more than one sentence.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** NOTE: The gyroscope will become stable after approximately 15 seconds.
**Good:** NOTE: It is not necessary to remove the nameplate (33) from the flange (27).
**Good:** NOTE: You can use equivalent alternatives for these items.
**Good:** NOTE: During the subsequent test, you get the cracking pressure when the fuel flow from the CROSS FEED port is more than 5 cc/minute. (One sentence, 22 words.)
**Good:** NOTE: The data collection is not completed. Thus, the statistics module can give incorrect results. (Two sentences, 6 words and 8 words.)

</EXAMPLE>

3. You **MUST** move information into a safety instruction when it is important to prevent damage or injury. A reader can miss what a note carries.
4. You **MUST** test a procedure that contains notes: read the procedure without the notes, and make sure that the reader can still do it correctly. If information is missing, remove it from the note, write it as a work step, put that step where it belongs, and test again.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** give an instruction or a requirement in a note. The imperative form turns a note into a work step.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** NOTE: Make sure that the avionics ventilation system continues to operate correctly. (This is not a note because it contains the imperative form.)
**Good:** (6) Make sure that the avionics ventilation system continues to operate correctly.

</EXAMPLE>

2. You **MUST NOT** write notes in descriptions, except where a note is necessary for an illustration or a table that is part of the description.

## Safety instructions

A warning tells the reader that there is a risk of injury or death. A caution tells the reader that there is a risk of damage to objects. Other words — "danger", "attention", "notice" — or graphical symbols are available if the content still obeys these three rules. The examples below are in uppercase letters, but case and formatting are a matter for the applicable style guide, not for these rules.

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** do an accurate risk analysis to identify the level of risk, then decide. A risk of injury or death to persons takes a warning. A risk of damage to machines, tools, or equipment takes a caution. The two levels together take a warning.
2. You **MUST** use a word, or where applicable a symbol, that shows the reader the level of risk immediately.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** WARNING: BEFORE YOU FILL THE LIQUID OXYGEN SYSTEM, PUT ON A FACE MASK AND PROTECTIVE CLOTHING. LIQUID OXYGEN CAN CAUSE IRRITATION OF THE RESPIRATORY TRACT AND EYE IRRITATION.

**Good:** WARNING: ALWAYS KEEP YOUR HANDS AND FEET AWAY FROM THE BLADE. WHEN THE MOTOR OPERATES, THE BLADE TURNS AND CAN CAUSE INJURY.

**Good:** CAUTION: BEFORE YOU OPERATE THE GROUND TEST UNIT, MAKE SURE THAT THE PRESSURE REGULATOR IS SET TO ZERO. THIS WILL PREVENT DAMAGE TO THE UNIT.

**Good:** CAUTION: DO NOT USE BLEACH OR CLEANSERS THAT CONTAIN CHLORINE TO CLEAN THE UNIT. THESE CLEANING AGENTS CAN CAUSE CORROSION.

</EXAMPLE>

3. You **MUST** start a safety instruction with a clear and simple command or condition, so the reader knows what to do to prevent an accident and keep a high level of safety.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** WARNING: DO NOT SWALLOW THE SOLVENT. ALWAYS MAKE SURE THAT YOU KNOW THE SAFETY PRECAUTIONS AND FIRST AID INSTRUCTIONS FOR SOLVENTS. SOLVENTS ARE POISONOUS AND CAN CAUSE INJURY OR DEATH TO PERSONNEL.

**Good:** CAUTION: DO NOT USE BLEACH OR CLEANSERS THAT CONTAIN CHLORINE TO CLEAN THE UNIT. THESE CLEANING AGENTS CAN CAUSE CORROSION.

</EXAMPLE>

4. You **MUST** give the condition first when the reader must know a specific condition before the procedure or work step starts.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** WARNING: WHILE YOU USE THE SPRAY PAINT, POINT THE SPRAY AWAY FROM YOUR FACE. THE SPRAY PAINT CAN CAUSE INJURY TO YOUR EYES.

**Good:** CAUTION: WHEN YOU ASSEMBLE THE UNIT, DO NOT LET THE PARTS FALL. IF THEY FALL, PERMANENT DAMAGE TO THE PARTS CAN OCCUR.

</EXAMPLE>

5. You **MUST**, if possible, give an explanation that shows the specific risk or the possible result. A reader who knows the danger will be more careful. In the four examples above, the explanation is the sentence that names what can occur — poisoning, corrosion, injury to the eyes, permanent damage to the parts.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** write a safety instruction that is abstract or that makes only a general statement. Name the risk in plain words — explosion, injury, death, corrosion, damage.

</EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>
