# Communication Specimens — Instructions and Safety

<EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>

Passage-length before-and-after pairs whose good version is a numbered work-step sequence, a note, or a safety instruction. A single example shows one fault. A specimen shows how faults compound across a passage, and how the repair reads. Every passage-length pair lives in exactly one specimen document and is never repeated beside its rule. A short example that the source gives under two different rules does appear under both. The rules are in the guidelines documents.

## Short and clear sentences

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** To remove the cover assembly (9), first remove the four screws (10) that attach the cover (11) to the housing (12), and then, after taking the cover (11) off the housing (12), remove the preformed packing (13) and throw it away.

**Good:**
1. Remove the cover assembly (9) as follows:
   A. Remove the four screws (10) that attach the cover (11) to the housing (12).
   B. Remove the cover (11) from the housing (12).
   C. Remove and discard the preformed packing (13).

</EXAMPLE>

## One name for one item

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:**
1. Make sure that the servo control unit is in the open position.
2. Do the operational test of the actuator.
3. Disconnect the control unit from the test rig.

**Good:**
1. Make sure that the actuator is in the open position.
2. Do the operational test of the actuator.
3. Disconnect the actuator from the test rig.

("Servo control unit", "actuator", and "control unit" all refer to the same item.)

</EXAMPLE>

## An action not described by a verb

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Make sure that the 600 Hz tone is audible when the GPW GND ACT switch is pushed.

**Good:**
(a) Push the GPW GND ACT switch.
(b) Make sure that you can hear the 600 Hz tone at the same time.

</EXAMPLE>

## One wording for one recurring step

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:**
1. Put the housing (8) in its installation position on the main body (9).
2. Lubricate the two bolts (10) with oil.
3. Install the two bolts (10) in the body (9).
4. Torque-tighten the two bolts to 4 Nm.
5. Put the transducer (11) on the body assembly (9).
6. Apply a small quantity of oil to the threads of the four bolts (12).
7. Attach the transducer (11) to the body assembly (9) with the four bolts (12).
8. Torque each of the four bolts (12) to 0.8 Nm.

**Good:**
1. Put the housing (8) in its installation position on the body assembly (9).
2. Apply a small quantity of oil to the threads of the two bolts (10).
3. Install the two bolts (10) in the body assembly (9).
4. Torque each of the two bolts to 4 Nm.
5. Put the transducer (11) on the body assembly (9).
6. Apply a small quantity of oil to the threads of the four bolts (12).
7. Attach the transducer (11) to the body assembly (9) with the four bolts (12).
8. Torque each of the four bolts (12) to 0.8 Nm.

(The bad version names item (9) three ways — "main body", "body", and "body assembly" — uses two wordings for tightening the bolts, and two different sentences for the lubrication steps.)

</EXAMPLE>

## Safety information filed as a note

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** NOTE: When you connect the lines, do not bend them too much. If you bend the lines too much, you can cause damage to them. (This is not a note. It is a safety instruction.)

**Good:** CAUTION: WHEN YOU CONNECT THE LINES, DO NOT BEND THEM TOO MUCH. IF YOU BEND THE LINES TOO MUCH, YOU CAN CAUSE DAMAGE TO THEM.

</EXAMPLE>

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** NOTE: Before closing the hatch, it is mandatory to ensure that no persons are in the crew rest compartment. When the hatch is closed, there is no airflow to the compartment and therefore there is a risk of suffocation. (Although the text does not contain the imperative form, it is not a note. It is a safety instruction.)

**Good:** WARNING: BEFORE YOU CLOSE THE HATCH, MAKE SURE THAT NO PERSONS ARE IN THE CREW REST COMPARTMENT. WHEN THE HATCH IS CLOSED, THERE IS NO AIRFLOW TO THE COMPARTMENT AND THERE IS A RISK OF SUFFOCATION.

</EXAMPLE>

## A hazard buried under "-ing" clauses

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Mechanics wearing insufficient protective clothing and opening containers containing hazardous materials in areas where there is a lack of ventilation, using inappropriate tools without observing the manufacturer's instructions, are in danger of coming into contact with these materials and thus suffering from skin irritation and breathing problems.

**Good:** Before you use dangerous materials, obey these precautions:
(1) Read the manufacturer's instructions.
(2) Make sure that there is sufficient airflow in the work area.
(3) Put on a face mask and protective clothing.
(4) Get the correct tools to open the containers for these materials.

If you do not obey these precautions, injury to your skin and lungs can occur.

</EXAMPLE>

## The wrong level of risk, named abstractly

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** CAUTION: EXTREME CLEANLINESS OF OXYGEN TUBES IS IMPERATIVE.

**Good:** WARNING: MAKE SURE THAT THE OXYGEN TUBES ARE FULLY CLEAN. OXYGEN AND OIL OR GREASE MAKE AN EXPLOSIVE MIXTURE. AN EXPLOSION CAN CAUSE INJURY OR DEATH TO PERSONNEL.

(Oxygen mixed with other materials can cause an explosion, so the risk is injury or death and the instruction must be a warning. The bad version is abstract and makes only a general statement. The good version names the explosion, the injury, and the death.)

</EXAMPLE>
