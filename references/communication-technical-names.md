# Communication Guidelines — Technical Names

<EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>

A technical name is a word from the subject's own vocabulary, outside the common vocabulary. These rules say which words qualify and how to use them. The companion document covers technical verbs. The passage-length before-and-after pairs are in the specimen documents.

## Technical names

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** treat a word as a technical name only when it falls in one or more of these categories. The listed words are examples, not a complete list.
   1. Names in the official parts information (for example, Illustrated Parts Catalog/List or engineering drawing): bolt, cable, clip, engine, filter, screw, switch
   2. Names of vehicles or machines, and locations on them: aircraft, cabin, cockpit, fuselage, ship, wing
   3. Names of tools and support equipment, their parts, and locations on them: blade, cap, cover, gauge, handle, tag
   4. Names of materials, consumables, and unwanted material: adhesive, debris, dust, fuel, grease, oil, paint, water
   5. Names of facilities, infrastructure, and logistic procedures: airport, base, building, hangar, packaging, shop, storage
   6. Names of systems, components and circuits, their functions, configurations, and parts: exhaust, hardware, inlet, latch, pump, standby mode
   7. Mathematical, scientific, engineering terms, and formulas: average, category, cycle, defect, density, energy, failure, load, pressure, “C = (A - B) - 0.063 mm”
   8. Navigation and geographic terms: air, altitude, axis, clearance, deviation, heading, north
   9. Numbers, units of measurement and time (and their symbols): 92, half, hour (h), kilogram (kg), meter (m), one, year
   10. Quoted text (for example, that on placards, labels, signs, markings, and display units): EXIT sign, ON position, NEXT button, FAULT legend
   11. Names of persons, groups, or organizations: captain, crew, manufacturer, operator
   12. Parts of the body: blood, ear, eyes, hand, head, lung, skin
   13. Common personal effects, food, and drink: bread, clothing, coffee, footwear, milk, mineral water
   14. Medical terms: allergy, asthma, breathing, dizziness, irritation, medication, pulse
   15. Names of official documents and parts of documentation (this includes manuals, technical records, standards, specifications, and regulations): caution, chapter, Checklist, diagram, figure, note, page, paragraph, section, table, warning
   16. Environmental and operational conditions: atmosphere, cloud, day, ice, humidity, night, rain, snow, wind
   17. Colors: black, gray, green, orange, red, white, yellow. Colors are adjectives, but by convention they count as technical names. Comparative and superlative forms of colors — for example blacker, the reddest — are not permitted.
   18. Damage terms: buckle, corrosion, crack, dent, distortion, erosion, scratch, stain
   19. Information technology and telephony terms: backup, backup file, cursor, database, file, icon, internet, laptop, memory, menu, network, operating system, screen, smartphone, update
   20. Military terms: armed forces, bomb, checkpoint, deployment, evacuation, mission, patrol, rank
2. You **MUST** follow the practice these lists show. A technical name has capital letters only where they are necessary — for example for units of measurement, titles, official names, or quoted text.
3. You **MUST** use a word you would otherwise avoid only when it is a technical name or part of one, and only in that sense. The same word can fall in different categories in different contexts.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** The base of the triangle is 5 cm. ("Base" is a mathematical, scientific, or engineering term.)
**Bad:** Make sure that the two spigots at the base of the unit engage. ("Base" here is not available, because it is related to a surface.)
**Good:** Make sure that the two spigots at the bottom of the unit engage.
**Good:** Access to the base is permitted between 9 a.m. and 6 p.m. ("Base" is a name of a facility.)

**Good:** Do the backup of the computer at regular intervals.
**Good:** Keep the backup file in a safe area.
**Bad:** For this procedure, make sure that one person is available as backup. ("Backup" is not available here and you cannot use it.)
**Good:** Two persons are necessary to do this procedure.

**Bad:** The laptop has these main parts: ("Main part" is not a technical name, and it is correct to replace "main" with "primary".)
**Good:** The laptop has these primary parts:
**Good:** Retract the main landing gear. ("Main landing gear" is a technical name. Do not replace "main" with "primary" here, because "primary landing gear" is not the official name given.)

</EXAMPLE>

4. You **MUST** use a technical name that agrees with the subject's established nomenclature. If a designated name exists for a system, component, part, or process, use that name.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** The front panel of the phone has a touchscreen and a home button.

</EXAMPLE>

5. You **MUST** select a technical name that is short and easy to understand when nomenclature gives none, and you **MUST** make sure it is a well-known word.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Remove the four stainless steel pan head machine screws (10) that attach the metallic machined flange (15) to the front housing cover (20).
**Good:** Remove the four screws (10) that attach the flange (15) to the cover (20).
(The words "screws", "flange", and "cover" are sufficient here, because these parts have index numbers and the related illustration identifies them clearly.)

</EXAMPLE>

6. You **MUST** use one technical name for one item, in every part of the text and throughout your documentation.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** use a technical name as a verb. Use it as a noun, or as an adjective that is part of a technical name, and find a different construction for the action.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Oil the steel surfaces.
**Good:** Apply oil to the steel surfaces.

**Bad:** If you think it will snow, make sure that the vehicle is in the applicable configuration.
**Good:** If you think that snow will fall, make sure that the vehicle is in the applicable configuration.

</EXAMPLE>

2. You **MUST NOT** use slang or jargon as a technical name. A word used in one geographical area, or understood by very few people in one technical area, will cause confusion.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Make a sandwich with two washers and the spacer.
**Good:** Install the spacer between the two washers.

**Bad:** Remove your gear from the work area.
**Good:** Remove your tools and equipment from the work area.

</EXAMPLE>

3. You **MUST NOT** use a different technical name for the same item — never "servo control unit", "actuator", and "control unit" for one part.

</EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>
