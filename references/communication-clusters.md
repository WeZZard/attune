# Communication Guidelines — Noun Clusters

<EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>

Rules for groups of nouns, and for the articles and demonstrative adjectives that go before them. The specimen documents carry the passage-length before-and-after pairs.

## Noun clusters

A noun cluster is a group of words that works as one part of speech in a sentence, usually as the subject or the object, and is made of nouns and adjectives — "Horizontal cylinder pivot bearing", "Actuator operating rod". The main noun, or head, is usually the last word. A long cluster is hard to read because its words can connect to each other in more than one way, and because in some languages the main noun comes first.

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** write noun clusters of no more than three words. Articles and prepositions do not count toward the three. Where a long cluster comes whole from the subject's established nomenclature, and cannot be divided, write it as it is.
2. You **MUST** open out a cluster that is too long. You can use prepositions to do it — for example "of", "on", "in", and "for".

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Runway light connection resistance calibration.
**Good:** Calibration of the resistance of the runway light connection.

</EXAMPLE>

3. You **MUST** write a technical name of more than three words in full the first time it occurs. Then, if possible, use a shorter name or its established abbreviation in the rest of the document, or hyphenate the words that work as one unit.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** Inspection of the lavatory rapid-decompression device.

</EXAMPLE>

4. You **MUST** keep a hyphenated group to three words or fewer. Hyphenating every word does not help the reader.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Main-gear-door-retraction-winch handle.
**Good:** Main-gear-door retraction-winch handle.

</EXAMPLE>

5. You **MUST** leave a technical name that comes from established nomenclature with its hyphens unchanged. If it is too long, write it in full the first time it occurs, then use the shorter name after that.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** connect words that are not related when you hyphenate, because that changes the meaning of the cluster. If you are not sure, explain the cluster instead, then use a shorter version or an established abbreviation.

## Articles and demonstrative adjectives

Articles and demonstrative adjectives show the reader where nouns and noun clusters are.

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** put an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative adjective (this, these, that, those) before a noun or a noun cluster where one applies, and you **MUST NOT** drop it to shorten the text.

<EXAMPLE>

**Bad:** Turn shaft assembly.
**Good:** Turn the shaft assembly.

</EXAMPLE>

2. You **MUST** omit the article in a general statement, and you **MUST** omit the definite article before a noun that an alphanumeric identifier follows. In a short sentence it can be clearer to use articles before all nouns. A sentence that contains a long series of items is clearer when you do not repeat the articles.

<EXAMPLE>

**Good:** Solvents can cause damage to paint.

</EXAMPLE>

</EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>
