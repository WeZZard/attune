# Communication Guidelines — General Recommendations

<EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>

## General recommendations

These are not rules. They catch mistakes that writers make frequently.

1. **The conjunction "that".** It can prevent ambiguity, so try not to omit it. It shows the reader where the main clause ends and the subordinate clause starts, and it helps translation, because many languages cannot omit the equivalent word.

<EXAMPLE>

**Do not write:** Make sure the valve is open.
**WRITE:** Make sure that the valve is open.

**Do not write:** The manufacturer recommends you prepare the mixture in an area with good airflow.
**WRITE:** The manufacturer recommends that you prepare the mixture in an area with good airflow.

**Do not write:** The gauge shows the reservoir is full.
**WRITE:** The gauge shows that the reservoir is full.

</EXAMPLE>

2. **The preposition "with".** It shows association or relationship, help or sharing, or a means or instrument — and it can cause ambiguity. "Install the panel with the green fasteners" can mean install the panel that has green fasteners, install the panel together with the green fasteners, or use the green fasteners to install the panel. Usually the context gives the reader enough to know which meaning is correct. But read any sentence with "with" again, and make sure it is not ambiguous, that there are no errors, and that it says what you wanted to say.

<EXAMPLE>

**Do not write:** Lift the aircraft at the maximum takeoff weight with passengers.
**WRITE:** Lift the aircraft at the maximum takeoff weight (passenger weight included.)

**Do not write:** Make sure that there is no contact with stop (1) with hydraulic pressure applied.
**WRITE:** When you apply hydraulic pressure, make sure that the lever does not touch the stop (1).

**Do not write:** Seal the opening with tool TS9867.
**WRITE:** Use tool TS9867 to seal the opening.
(Write this type of sentence only if you think that the sentence is ambiguous. The action verb is not "use", but "seal". You can write: Seal the opening. To do that, use tool TS9867.)

</EXAMPLE>

3. **How to use pronouns.** A pronoun refers back to a person, place, or thing already named — "it", "they", "that", "these", "those". Do not use a pronoun outside the settled set. "She" and "he" are not among them. A pronoun that can refer to more than one noun can make a sentence ambiguous. If the sentence is ambiguous, replace the pronoun with the word it refers to.

<EXAMPLE>

**Do not write:** If you engage the pins incorrectly with the seats, they can become damaged.
**WRITE:** If you engage the pins incorrectly with the seats, the pins can become damaged.
or
**WRITE:** If you engage the pins incorrectly with the seats, the seats can become damaged.
or
**WRITE:** If you engage the pins incorrectly with the seats, the pins and seats can become damaged.

</EXAMPLE>

4. **The pronoun "this".** Make sure the reader knows what it refers to. If it could refer to more than one thing, describe the context again.

<EXAMPLE>

**Do not write:** Make sure that the cover is not locked (this can cause damage to the probe.)
**WRITE:** Make sure that the cover is not locked. If the cover is locked, this can cause damage to the probe.
or
**WRITE:** If the cover is locked, damage to the probe can occur.

**Do not write:** Do not use crocus cloth on aluminum parts. If you do this, you can cause corrosion on aluminum parts. Crocus cloth contains ferrous oxide.
**WRITE:** Do not use crocus cloth on aluminum parts. Crocus cloth contains ferrous oxide and can cause corrosion on aluminum parts.

</EXAMPLE>

5. **False friends.** A false friend is a word that resembles one in another language but means something different — English "disposition" against Italian "disposizione" or Spanish "disposición". If you are a non-native English speaker, make sure the word carries its English meaning, not the meaning of the similar word in your native language.

<EXAMPLE>

**Do not write:** Obey the dispositions of the manufacturer when you use this adhesive.
**WRITE:** When you use this adhesive, obey the manufacturer’s instructions.

</EXAMPLE>

6. **Latin abbreviations.** We recommend that you do not use "e.g.", "i.e.", or "etc.", because they can confuse your readers. Always use English words. Frequently these abbreviations are not necessary and you can omit them.

<EXAMPLE>

**Do not write:** Discard the standard parts (e.g. washers, screws, bolts, and nuts) each time you remove them.
**WRITE:** Discard the standard parts (for example, washers, bolts, and nuts) each time you remove them.

**Do not write:** These wires can have insulation of different colors (blue, green, red, etc.)
**WRITE:** These wires can have insulation of different colors. They can be blue, green, red, or other colors.
or
**WRITE:** These wires can have insulation of different colors.

</EXAMPLE>

</EXTREMELY_IMPORTANT>
